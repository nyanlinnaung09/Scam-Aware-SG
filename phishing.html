Phishing

The fraudster comes up with a fake message that appears to come from the bank, government office, or other websites. This message will normally have information on how your account has a serious issue and a link that you need to use to verify your information.

How it works

The scammer creates a convincing message pretending to be from a bank, government agency, or online service. The message often claims there is an urgent problem with your account and provides a link to “verify” your information. The fake website then captures the details you enter.

Another case occurred in 2024 where a Singaporean became a victim of phishing attacks whereby he was cheated of his money through an advertisement of the attack on the TikTok application. In this case, the attackers obtained the details of the credit card as well as the one-time password and registered it in the Apple Pay wallet. Although he was receiving several alerts from the bank with regard to the fraudulent transactions, he did not take the warning seriously. He eventually suffered losses from 22 transactions carried out using Japanese yen. The bank eventually blocked the card; however, only S$355 could be recovered.

CNA

Why it's effective

Phishing scams look like genuine messages and use urgency or fear to make victims act quickly without checking the source.

How to avoid it

  • Check the sender and website address carefully.
  • Never share your password or OTP with anyone.
  • Contact the organisation directly using its official website or app.

E-commerce Scams

A scammer creates a fake online listing or store, offering attractive products at low prices, then tricks buyers into making payments without delivering the goods.

How it works

Scammers advertise counterfeit goods on online shopping websites or create fake websites. They might use popular goods that sell at very cheap rates to lure customers. Once they get paid, they might run away or give fake tracking numbers or send a product that is not as described.

Mr. Kamal, who is about in his sixties, has lost more than S$700 following his reaction to an online ad regarding cute toilet rolls in October 2024. This ad was convincing, and he was tricked into filling in his personal details along with those of his credit card. While the purchase was made successfully, he did not receive any of the toilet rolls. Approximately two weeks later, it came to his notice that there were unauthorized transactions totaling over $843, and these involved electronic payments from an Australian department store. His card was also enrolled into a strange Google Pay account.

THE STRAITS TIMES

Why it's effective

Low prices and limited-time offers can make victims rush into purchases without checking the seller. Fake reviews, stolen product images, and convincing profiles can also make fraudulent sellers appear legitimate.

How to avoid it

  • Check the seller's reviews and ratings before buying.
  • Be cautious of prices that seem too good to be true.
  • Do not click suspicious payment or delivery links.
  • Check the product and seller carefully before transferring money.

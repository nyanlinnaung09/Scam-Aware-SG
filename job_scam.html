Job Scams

Fake job offers promise easy work and high salaries, but may ask victims to pay fees, transfer money, or share personal information.

How it works

Scammers post fake job opportunities on social media, messaging apps, or job platforms. Victims are promised easy work and high earnings, but are asked to pay upfront fees, transfer money, or provide personal and banking information.

The Police arrested four men and a 17-year-old female for suspected involvement in job scams between March and May 2026. The scammers contacted victims through Carousell job advertisements and tricked them into providing their NRIC details and changing their Singpass phone numbers and email addresses. This allowed the scammers to take control of the accounts and use them to apply for financial and mobile services. Five suspects linked to an overseas scam syndicate were arrested on 1 July 2026. Four men will be charged under the computer misuse act, while investigations into the teenager continues.The Police remind the public to protect their Singpass credentials.

PUBLIC AFFAIRS DEPARTMENT

Why it's effective

The fake job offers usually seem to be legitimate jobs with imaginary company names, websites, and recruiters. The prospect of quick earnings may mislead the victims to ignore the red flags and transfer their money or personal details.

How to avoid it

  • Watch out for offers of extremely lucrative jobs that require little effort.
  • Do not make any advance payments to guarantee yourself a job or salary.
  • Always check the credibility of the company and the job offer on official sites.
